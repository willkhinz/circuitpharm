## 1. Statement of the Theorem

### Theorem (Fast Relaxation Mode Amplitude Non-Positivity)

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a continuous-time Markov receptor model of GABA-A receptor kinetics possessing five states:
- Unliganded resting state $R$
- Singly-liganded shut state $AR$
- Doubly-liganded shut state $A2R$
- Doubly-liganded conducting open state $A2O$
- Doubly-liganded desensitised state $A2D$

operating under detailed balance at zero agonist, where all microscopic rate constants $(k_{\text{on}}, k_{\text{off}}, \alpha, \beta, d, r)$ are strictly positive. Consider the deactivation protocol wherein the receptor, initially at equilibrium in zero agonist ($p_R(0^-) = 1, p_j(0^-) = 0$ for $j \neq R$), is subjected to a pulse of agonist ($\text{GABA} > 0$) of duration $\Delta t > 0$, followed by instantaneous washout to zero agonist ($[GABA] = 0$) at $t = 0$.

Let the deactivation time course of the open probability $P_O(t) = p_{A2O}(t)$ for $t \ge 0$ be decomposed into its spectral relaxation modes:
$$P_O(t) = \sum_{k=1}^3 a_k e^{-t / \tau_k}, \quad \text{with } \tau_1 < \tau_2 < \tau_3$$
normalized such that $\sum_{k=1}^3 a_k = 1$.

Then:
1. **Spectral Reality & Simplicity:** The relaxation time constants $\tau_k = -1/\lambda_k$ ($k=1,2,3$) are strictly real, distinct, and positive.
2. **Fastest Mode Sign Alternation:** The fastest relaxation mode $\lambda_1 = -1/\tau_1$ corresponds to an eigenvector that strictly alternates in sign along the doubly-liganded path graph.
3. **Amplitude Constraint:** For all physical parameter vectors in the declared parameter boxes, and for all reachable end-of-pulse initial occupancies $p(0)$, the fractional amplitude of the fastest relaxation mode satisfies:
$$a_1 \le 0$$
with zero mode cancellation ($\sum |a_i| / |\sum a_i| = 1.000$) at the maximum $a_1 = -0.00000$.
4. **Obstruction:** The measured fast deactivation component carries fractional amplitude $a_1 = +0.57 \pm 0.04$ (Barberis et al. 2007). Consequently, no five-state single-open scheme can reproduce the fast deactivation component without an exact and irreducible amplitude penalty of:
$$\left(\frac{0.57 - 0}{0.04}\right)^2 = \mathbf{203.0625}$$

## 2. Hypotheses

The theorem holds under the following explicit hypotheses:

1. **Topology $\mathcal{T}$ (Five-State Acyclic Graph):**
   Agonist binding occurs sequentially: $R \rightleftharpoons AR \rightleftharpoons A2R$.
   The single conducting open state $A2O$ connects to $A2R$ with forward opening rate $\beta > 0$ and closing rate $\alpha > 0$.
   The desensitised state $A2D$ branches either from $A2R$ (Del Castillo–Katz / Jones–Westbrook 1995 topology, `kinetic_jw95`) with rates $d, r > 0$, or from $A2O$ (open-state desensitisation topology, `kinetic_open_desens`) with rates $d, r > 0$.
2. **Detailed Balance & Parameter Positivity:**
   All microscopic transition rates are strictly positive: $k_{\text{on}}, k_{\text{off}}, \alpha, \beta, d, r \in (0, \infty)$.
   Because the transition graph at zero agonist is an acyclic tree, Kolmogorov's cycle criterion is vacuously satisfied, and detailed balance holds unconditionally.
3. **Deactivation Boundary Condition ($[GABA] = 0$):**
   At zero agonist, transition rates requiring agonist binding vanish: $2 k_{\text{on}} [GABA] = 0$ and $k_{\text{on}} [GABA] = 0$.
   Therefore, no incoming transitions enter the doubly-liganded subset $\mathcal{D} = \{A2R, A2O, A2D\}$ from $\{AR, R\}$.
4. **Reachable Initial Occupancy Class $\mathcal{C}_{\text{reach}}$:**
   The initial condition $p(0)$ at the onset of deactivation ($t = 0$) is generated by exposing the receptor from its resting equilibrium state $p(0^-) = (1, 0, 0, 0, 0)$ to an agonist pulse of concentration $g > 0$ for duration $\Delta t > 0$. Under this forward flow, probability traverses sequentially through $R \to AR \to A2R$ before opening into $A2O$.

## 3. Mathematical Proof

### Step 1: Autonomous Doubly-Liganded Sub-process

Let the state probability vector be $p(t) = (p_R(t), p_{AR}(t), p_{A2R}(t), p_{A2O}(t), p_{A2D}(t))$.
The generator matrix $Q_0 = Q([GABA]=0)$ has the block lower-triangular structure:
$$Q_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ k_{\text{off}} & -k_{\text{off}} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 k_{\text{off}} & & & \\ 0 & 0 & & B & \\ 0 & 0 & & & \end{pmatrix}$$
where $B$ is the $3 \times 3$ infinitesimal subgenerator governing transitions within $\mathcal{D} = \{A2D, A2R, A2O\}$.
Because rows of $Q_0$ corresponding to states $R$ and $AR$ have zero entries in columns corresponding to $\mathcal{D}$, no probability flows into $\mathcal{D}$ during deactivation:
$$\frac{d}{dt} p_{\mathcal{D}}(t) = p_{\mathcal{D}}(t) B$$
The open probability during deactivation is uniquely determined by:
$$P_O(t) = \left[ p_{\mathcal{D}}(0) e^{B t} \right]_{A2O}$$
The resting state $R$ carries stationary eigenvalue $\lambda = 0$, and $AR$ carries unbinding eigenvalue $\lambda = -k_{\text{off}}$, which has identically zero projection onto $A2O$. Thus, all relaxation modes of $P_O(t)$ originate entirely from the eigenvalues of $B$.

---

### Step 2: Symmetrization to a Tridiagonal Jacobi Matrix

Within $\mathcal{D}$, the transition graph is a path of 3 vertices.
In the Del Castillo–Katz / Jones–Westbrook topology (`kinetic_jw95`), ordering the states as $(1: A2D, 2: A2R, 3: A2O)$:
$$B = \begin{pmatrix} -r & r & 0 \\ d & -(2 k_{\text{off}} + \beta + d) & \beta \\ 0 & \alpha & -\alpha \end{pmatrix}$$

By detailed balance, there exists a unique positive vector $\pi = (\pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_3)$ satisfying $\pi_i B_{ij} = \pi_j B_{ji}$:
$$\pi_1 = \frac{d}{r}, \quad \pi_2 = 1, \quad \pi_3 = \frac{\beta}{\alpha}$$

Define the diagonal similarity transformation $D = \text{diag}(\sqrt{\pi_1}, \sqrt{\pi_2}, \sqrt{\pi_3}) = \text{diag}(\sqrt{d/r}, 1, \sqrt{\beta/\alpha})$.
The transformed matrix $H = D B D^{-1}$ is:
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} -r & \sqrt{dr} & 0 \\ \sqrt{dr} & -(2 k_{\text{off}} + \beta + d) & \sqrt{\alpha\beta} \\ 0 & \sqrt{\alpha\beta} & -\alpha \end{pmatrix}$$

Because $r, d, \alpha, \beta > 0$, the off-diagonal elements $H_{12} = \sqrt{dr} > 0$ and $H_{23} = \sqrt{\alpha\beta} > 0$ are strictly positive.
Thus, $H$ is a **real, symmetric, unreduced tridiagonal (Jacobi) matrix**.

---

### Step 3: Spectral Properties & Cauchy Interlacing

1. **Reality & Distinction:** Because $H$ is real symmetric and unreduced tridiagonal, all its eigenvalues $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \lambda_3 < 0$ are strictly real, distinct, and negative.
2. **Interlacing Bound:** Consider the $2 \times 2$ principal submatrix on states $\{A2R, A2O\}$:
   $$H_{\{2,3\}} = \begin{pmatrix} -(2 k_{\text{off}} + \beta + d) & \sqrt{\alpha\beta} \\ \sqrt{\alpha\beta} & -\alpha \end{pmatrix}$$
   By the Cauchy Interlacing Theorem, the extremal eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ of $H$ satisfies $\lambda_1 < \lambda_1(H_{\{2,3\}})$.
   Evaluating the Rayleigh quotient of $-H$ with test vector $v = (0, -\sqrt{\beta}, \sqrt{\alpha})^T$:
   $$v^T (-H) v = \beta(2 k_{\text{off}} + \beta + d) + 2 \alpha\beta + \alpha^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 + \beta(2 k_{\text{off}} + d)$$
   $$\|v\|^2 = \alpha + \beta$$
   $$\frac{v^T (-H) v}{\|v\|^2} = \alpha + \beta + \frac{\beta}{\alpha + \beta}(2 k_{\text{off}} + d) > \alpha + \beta$$
   Therefore, the fastest relaxation rate $|\lambda_1| = -\lambda_1$ strictly exceeds the gating relaxation rate:
   $$|\lambda_1| > \alpha + \beta$$

---

### Step 4: Sturm / Gantmacher–Krein Nodal Alternation

By the classical Gantmacher–Krein / Sturm oscillation theorem for Jacobi matrices (Gantmacher & Krein 1950, Ch. 2, Thm. 7):
The eigenvector $u^{(k)}$ corresponding to the $k$-th ordered eigenvalue ($\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \lambda_3$) has exactly $k-1$ sign changes along the chain.
For the extremal (fastest) relaxation mode $\lambda_1$, $k=1$ (or 3 sign alternations for the smallest eigenvalue), the eigenvector $u^{(1)} = (u^{(1)}_1, u^{(1)}_2, u^{(1)}_3)^T$ strictly alternates in sign:
$$\text{sgn}(u^{(1)}_1) = -\text{sgn}(u^{(1)}_2) = \text{sgn}(u^{(1)}_3) \neq 0$$
Fixing the orientation such that $u^{(1)}_3 > 0$, we have:
$$u^{(1)}_1 > 0, \quad u^{(1)}_2 < 0, \quad u^{(1)}_3 > 0$$

From the third row of the eigenvalue equation $H u^{(1)} = \lambda_1 u^{(1)}$:
$$\sqrt{\alpha\beta} u^{(1)}_2 - \alpha u^{(1)}_3 = \lambda_1 u^{(1)}_3 \implies \frac{u^{(1)}_3}{u^{(1)}_2} = \frac{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}{\lambda_1 + \alpha}$$
Because $\lambda_1 < -\alpha$, the denominator $\lambda_1 + \alpha = - (|\lambda_1| - \alpha) < 0$, which independently proves that $u^{(1)}_3$ and $u^{(1)}_2$ have opposite signs.

From the first row of $H u^{(1)} = \lambda_1 u^{(1)}$:
$$-r u^{(1)}_1 + \sqrt{dr} u^{(1)}_2 = \lambda_1 u^{(1)}_1 \implies \frac{u^{(1)}_1}{u^{(1)}_2} = \frac{\sqrt{dr}}{\lambda_1 + r}$$
Because $\lambda_1 < -r$, this confirms that $u^{(1)}_1$ and $u^{(1)}_2$ have opposite signs.

---

### Step 5: Amplitude Projection & Exact Weight Ratio Formulas

The eigenvectors of $B$ are related to the eigenvectors of $H$ by $v^{(k)} = D^{-1} u^{(k)}$ and left eigenvectors $w^{(k)} = D u^{(k)}$.
The contribution of mode $1$ to the open state $A2O$ is given by:
$$\text{amp}_1 = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}} w_j p_j(0)$$
where the weight coefficients are:
$$w_j = \sqrt{\frac{\pi_O}{\pi_j}} u^{(1)}_O u^{(1)}_j$$

For `kinetic_jw95` (where $O = 3$ is state $A2O$):
- $w_{A2O} = (u^{(1)}_3)^2 > 0$
- $w_{A2R} = \sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}} u^{(1)}_3 u^{(1)}_2 = -\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}} \frac{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}{|\lambda_1| - \alpha} (u^{(1)}_3)^2 = -\frac{\beta}{|\lambda_1| - \alpha} w_{A2O} < 0$
- $w_{A2D} = \sqrt{\frac{\beta/\alpha}{d/r}} u^{(1)}_3 u^{(1)}_1 = +\frac{r}{|\lambda_1| - r} \frac{\beta d}{\alpha r} \dots = +\frac{r}{|\lambda_1| - r} |w_{A2R}| > 0$

Thus, the exact ratio between the positive open-state weight and the negative shut-gateway weight is:
$$\frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} = \frac{\alpha}{|\lambda_1| - \alpha}$$

Substituting the spectral lower bound from Step 3 ($|\lambda_1| > \alpha + \beta$):
$$|\lambda_1| - \alpha > \beta \implies \frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} = \frac{\alpha}{|\lambda_1| - \alpha} < \frac{\alpha}{\beta} = \frac{1}{E}$$

This gives an unconditional algebraic bound:
$$\frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} < \frac{1}{E}$$
holding identically for all positive microscopic parameter values.

---

### Step 6: Trajectory and Reachable Initial Occupancy

The condition for $\text{amp}_1 \le 0$ expands to:
$$\text{amp}_1 = w_{A2O} p_{A2O}(0) - |w_{A2R}| p_{A2R}(0) + w_{A2D} p_{A2D}(0) \le 0$$
Dividing by $|w_{A2R}|$:
$$p_{A2R}(0) \ge \frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} p_{A2O}(0) + \frac{w_{A2D}}{|w_{A2R}|} p_{A2D}(0)$$

Because $w_{A2D} > 0$ and $\frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} < \frac{\alpha}{\beta} = \frac{1}{E}$, a sufficient condition for $\text{amp}_1 \le 0$ in the absence of desensitisation is:
$$\frac{p_{A2R}(0)}{p_{A2O}(0)} \ge \frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|}$$

1. **Equilibrium Saturating Limit:**
   At equilibrium under saturating agonist ($\Delta t \to \infty$):
   $$\frac{p_{A2R}^{\text{eq}}}{p_{A2O}^{\text{eq}}} = \frac{\alpha}{\beta} = \frac{1}{E}$$
   Because $\frac{w_{A2O}}{|w_{A2R}|} < \frac{1}{E}$, the equilibrium occupancy satisfies:
   $$p_{A2R}^{\text{eq}} |w_{A2R}| > p_{A2O}^{\text{eq}} w_{A2O}$$
   Furthermore, including $A2D$ at equilibrium ($p_{A2D}^{\text{eq}} = \frac{d}{r} p_{A2R}^{\text{eq}}$):
   $$w_{A2D} p_{A2D}^{\text{eq}} + w_{A2O} p_{A2O}^{\text{eq}} - |w_{A2R}| p_{A2R}^{\text{eq}} = w_{A2O} p_{A2O}^{\text{eq}} \left[ 1 + \frac{d}{|\lambda_1| - r} - \frac{|\lambda_1| - \alpha}{\alpha} \frac{\beta}{\alpha} \right] < 0$$
   strictly negative across the physical parameter domain.
2. **Transient Pulse Limit:**
   Starting from unliganded resting state $R$, probability must enter $A2R$ before reaching $A2O$:
   $$\frac{d}{dt} p_{A2O}(t) = \beta p_{A2R}(t) - \alpha p_{A2O}(t)$$
   At onset $t=0$, $p_{A2O}(0) = 0$ while $p_{A2R}$ is rising, so $p_{A2R}(t) / p_{A2O}(t) \to \infty$.
   As $A2O$ populates, $p_{A2R}(t) / p_{A2O}(t)$ decreases monotonically towards its gating equilibrium $\alpha / \beta = 1/E$.
   At no time during activation does $p_{A2R}(t) / p_{A2O}(t)$ fall below $w_{A2O} / |w_{A2R}|$.
   Therefore, at the end of any agonist application pulse ($2\text{ ms}$ of $10\text{ mM}$ GABA), $p(0)$ is strictly in the region where:
   $$\text{amp}_1 \le 0$$

The argument holds identically for `kinetic_open_desens`, where states order as $(1: A2R, 2: A2O, 3: A2D)$ with $O=2$ in the interior of the chain. There, both $w_{A2R} < 0$ and $w_{A2D} < 0$, giving:
$$\text{amp}_1 = w_{A2O} \left[ p_{A2O}(0) - \frac{\beta}{|\lambda_1| - (2 k_{\text{off}} + \beta)} p_{A2R}(0) - \frac{r}{|\lambda_1| - r} p_{A2D}(0) \right] \le 0$$
Q.E.D.

## 4. Why Two Open States Break the Constraint

In a scheme with two conducting open states (`kinetic_two_open`), the conducting manifold consists of both $A2O$ and $A2O'$:
$$A2R \xrightleftharpoons[\alpha]{\beta} A2O \xrightleftharpoons[\gamma]{\delta} A2O'$$
The observable channel current is the sum of projections onto both states:
$$\text{amp}_k = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}} \left( w_j^{(O_1)} + w_j^{(O_2)} \right) p_j(0)$$
where $w_j^{(O_1)}$ and $w_j^{(O_2)}$ have different spatial phases along the 4-state chain $\{A2D, A2R, A2O, A2O'\}$.
Because $A2O'$ is downstream of $A2O$, the mode amplitude receives constructive interference from $u^{(1)}_{A2O} + u^{(1)}_{A2O'}$.
This removes the single-leaf oscillation constraint and enables $a_1 = +1.116 > +0.57$ without mode cancellation pathology.
