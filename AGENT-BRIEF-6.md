## 1. Correction to `REVIEW-BRIEF-5.md` — my error, so you don't chase it

`REVIEW-BRIEF-5.md` claims the 6-parameter enlarged fits are failed optimisations, because
they report a **larger paired-pulse σ miss** than the 3-parameter model they contain
(+12.16σ vs +7.14σ). **That inference is invalid, and two of the numbers quoted in support
of it were wrong.**

**What is true:** `kinetic_jw95_free_rates` does strictly contain `kinetic_jw95`. Measured in
a clean process, at `(log10_kd, log10_E, log10_D) = (-0.428, 0.104, 1.9721)` on
Deact + Recovery:

| evaluated as | total log-likelihood |
|---|---|
| `kinetic_jw95` (3 params) | **−2.435202** |
| same point embedded in `kinetic_jw95_free_rates` (6 params) | **−2.435225** |

They agree to 2.3e-5 nats, and the two factories' rate constants agree to 1e-16
(`q_matrix` max abs difference 2.8e-14). **The embedding is exact and nesting holds.**

**Where I went wrong:** I compared a *per-dataset σ residual* across two models and read a
worse residual as a worse fit. It is not. Nesting constrains the **total** objective, not any
one component. An enlarged model is free to reallocate error between datasets, and under this
diagnostic's scoring it is strongly rewarded for doing so (§2).

**Also wrong in that review:** I quoted the convention values as `log10_koff = −0.477` and
`log10_alpha = −0.967`. The real values are **−0.743727** (`koff` = 0.180415) and
**−0.522879** (`alpha` = 0.30). The conclusion I drew from them — both inside the 6-parameter
box — happens to be correct, but the numbers were not generated by a script, which is exactly
the failure mode rule 3 exists to prevent. Correcting the record is Task A.

## 2. The real defect — the recovery point is cheap, and nobody decided that

`src/circuitpharm/fitting/diagnostic.py`, `diagnostic_log_likelihood`, scores each dataset by
its own declared `noise_model`:

* **`BARBERIS2007_DEACT`** — `noise_model="estimated"`, **~100 time points**, σ **profiled
  out**: `sigma2_hat = rss/n`, `ll = -0.5*n*(log(2π) + 1 + log(sigma2))`. Halving this
  dataset's RMSE gains **n·ln 2 ≈ 69 nats**, with no ceiling.
* **`BARBERIS2007_PAIRED_PULSE`** — `noise_model="stated_sem"`, **1 scalar point**, σ fixed
  at the published SEM of **0.03**: `ll = -0.5*(rss/σ² + log(2πσ²))`. Going from a 7σ miss to
  a 12σ miss costs **(144 − 49)/2 ≈ 47 nats**.

**69 > 47.** So the optimiser is *correctly* buying deactivation fit by sacrificing the
recovery point. The enlarged model, having more freedom, exploits this harder — which is
precisely why its σ miss is larger while its total likelihood is better. **The "+12.16σ" is
not evidence of a failed fit, and it is not evidence of an irreconcilable conflict either. It
is the objective function's exchange rate between a 100-point trace with free σ and a
1-point scalar with fixed σ.**

That exchange rate is an **assumption**, not a measurement. It was never declared, never
justified, and the brief-5 verdict ("neither topology and no combination of freed
microscopic rates can reconcile them") is stated as a property of the data and the model
structure when it is partly a property of this weighting. That is the recurring **E27**
failure mode — a protocol- or convention-dependent quantity reported as if it were
protocol-independent.

**The question this brief exists to answer:**

> Is the Barberis deactivation / paired-pulse recovery conflict a property of the data and
> the model structure, or an artefact of how the diagnostic weighs a 100-point trace with
> profiled σ against a 1-point scalar with a fixed SEM?
